# Icepack: bring your own problem with a coupled +15 m thickness change

Ask a concrete question: **what changes after one native step when the entire
synthetic shelf's thickness and its inflow thickness both increase by 15 m?**
The 322-second silent walkthrough builds this controlled input variant and
compares it with the retained [baseline HDF5 run](upload_dataset.md).

Start with [Icepack Basic](basic_icepack.md), an approved login and your reviewed
[BYO AWS connection](byo_aws_setup.md). The connected account pays its own charges;
the [internal pilot budget records](pilot_readiness.md) do not activate hosted
execution. This is an Icepack workflow and does not require a MATLAB license.

## What changes and what stays fixed

Every source-grid thickness value increases by **15 m**. Base and surface are
updated consistently with flotation: at every native scalar node, base changes
by about **−13.380350 m** and surface by **+1.619650 m**. Coordinates, floating
mask, initial vx/vy, scripts, image/resources, threading and observed MPI controls
stay fixed. Source data have shape **5 × 9** over a **10 km square**.

This is a **coupled interior-and-inflow thickness change**. The same initialized
`h0` field supplies interior thickness and the prognostic inflow thickness, so
the experiment does not isolate an interior-only perturbation. Native initial
thickness differs by exactly 15 m at all 25 nodes; independent interpolation of
all five thickness/base/surface/vx/vy fields has maximum error 0 in both runs.
Flotation and surface-closure errors in the stored initial arrays are 0.

## Compare the retained native solutions

Both executions use the same **25-vertex, 32-triangle** mesh over
**100,000,000 m²**, with exact x/y/connectivity equality. They take one
**0.01-year prognostic step** between diagnostic velocity solves, with zero
accumulation and no damage physics. Exported fields are finite and final
thickness is positive. Initial velocity is exactly equal between the two runs.
